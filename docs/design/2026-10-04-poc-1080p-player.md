# Proof of concept: one playable character at 1080p

**Date:** 2026-10-04. **Agreed with:** Ben. **Issue:** #17.

**This decides nothing.** It is a playable test of positions that are still **Proposed** in `docs/architecture.md`: three lanes (section 2), motion inputs and the button layout (section 5), and a 1920 by 1080 screen with large sprites (section 9). The team plays it, then decides at a weekly meeting.

It is built on the parts that are decided (`docs/design/2026-10-03-combat-core.md`): 60 ticks a second, moves as data, and a fighter body with explicit states.

## What it does

An empty stage with three lanes and one character who can:

| Action | Keyboard | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | A, D or the arrows | Stick or d-pad |
| Jump | W, K or Space | Up, or B |
| Crouch | S | Down |
| Change lane | Q (back), E (front) | Left and right shoulder |
| Light attack | J | X |
| Heavy attack (launches) | L | Y |
| Air attacks | J or L during a jump | X or Y during a jump |
| Fireball | Down, down-forward, forward, then light attack | The same |
| Show hitboxes and hurtboxes | F1 | |

The heavy attack, the air attacks and the training dummy came after this spec: see `docs/design/2026-10-04-hits-landing.md`. The same table is in `README.md`: keep the two in step.

Run the project (F5). The main scene is `scenes/levels/poc_stage/poc_stage.tscn`.

## How it is built

- **Clock.** The stage ticks the fighter and the projectiles from `_physics_process`, in a fixed order. Every duration is a number of ticks.
- **Input** (`systems/input/`). A `PlayerInput` belongs to a player number and reads an `InputSource`: a device, or a scripted list for tests and replays. It keeps 30 ticks of history. A button press stays usable for 8 ticks, so an attack pressed just before landing comes out on landing. `CommandReader` finds a motion in the history, relative to the way the fighter faces.
- **Moves** (`systems/combat/move.gd`, `data/moves/poc/`). A move is a `.tres` resource: animation, length, button, motion, hitboxes, and an optional projectile. The fighter tries its moves in order, so moves with a motion come first.
- **Fighter** (`scenes/fighter/fighter.gd`). States: idle, walk, crouch, jump, attack, lane change. Position is x, a lane and a height. It never reads a device.
- **Projectiles** (`systems/combat/projectiles.gd`). One pooled system with plain arrays, drawn by one node.

Not built yet: enemies that fight back, cancels between moves, block and dodge, and co-op. Hits landing on a training dummy came after, in `docs/design/2026-10-04-hits-landing.md`.

## Skins: how the art is swapped

The fighter is drawn by `FighterView`. If a skin is found it draws the skin's frames. If not, it draws a plain figure in code, so the game always runs without any art.

A skin is a folder with two files:

- `sheet.png`: every frame in a grid of equal cells.
- `skin.json`:

```json
{
  "sheet": "sheet.png",
  "cell": [646, 418],
  "feet": [323, 353],
  "columns": 8,
  "faces": "left",
  "animations": {
    "idle":     {"frames": [0, 1, 2, 1], "ticks": 7, "loop": true},
    "walk":     {"frames": [4, 5, 6, 7], "ticks": 4, "loop": true},
    "crouch":   {"frames": [8], "ticks": 8, "loop": true},
    "jump":     {"frames": [9, 10, 11], "ticks": 5, "loop": false},
    "light":    {"frames": [12, 13, 14], "ticks": 3, "loop": false},
    "fireball": {"frames": [15, 16, 17], "ticks": 5, "loop": false}
  }
}
```

- `cell` is the size of one cell, and `columns` how many cells are in a row. Frames are numbered from 0, left to right, top to bottom.
- `feet` is the point in a cell that sits on the fighter's position: between the feet, on the ground.
- `faces` says which way the drawing looks, `"left"` or `"right"`.
- An attack's frames are spread over the move's length, so `ticks` is ignored for them. A jump's frames follow the rise and the fall.
- `land` is optional. If a skin has it, it plays once when the fighter touches down from a jump and stays standing. It is only a look: the fighter can already move and attack, and doing so ends it.

How to draw or prepare frames so they sit right in the game, and where the feet point goes in each kind of frame, is in `docs/skins.md`.

The proof of concept looks for a skin in `local/skins/player/`. The `local/` folder is git-ignored and hidden from Godot's importer: it is the place for private placeholder art that must not be published, and it is where the hand-drawn sprite can be tried before it is committed properly under `assets/sprites/`.

A built game has no `local/` folder. It looks for the same two files in a `skins/player/` folder beside its executable, so art can be tried in a build without being packed into the game. `scripts/build.sh --skin <folder>` puts a skin there and names the zips `-private`. Only a build made without `--skin` goes on GitHub, unless everything in the skin may be published.

## Changes outside the proof of concept

- The project's viewport is now 1920 by 1080 (proposed, not decided), stretched to the window with the aspect kept.
- The crowd stress test (`scenes/stress_test/`) was made for 480 by 270. It is now shown through a 4 times zoom and is otherwise unchanged.

## Checks

`godot --headless --path . -s tests/poc_player_test.gd` feeds the fighter scripted inputs and checks walking, jumping, crouching, lane changes, the attack and its hitbox, the fireball in both directions, and the buffered press.
